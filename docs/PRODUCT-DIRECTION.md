# Student Rankz: product and growth direction

**Recommendation:** “Choose where to study. Know what studying there is really like.”

Build an EU-wide student-experience product with **university reviews and comparisons as the entry point**, and **course/teaching reviews as the reason enrolled students return**. This is a product hypothesis to test, not a proven growth formula.

## Adapt to programmes, not country stereotypes

| Official example | What the product needs to handle |
|---|---|
| [TUM Informatics, Germany](https://www.cit.tum.de/en/cit/studies/degree-programs/bachelor-informatics/) | Required foundations and elective study. |
| [Bologna Informatics, Italy](https://corsi.unibo.it/laurea/informatica/insegnamenti/piano-didattico) | Programme/year-specific study plans and choice requirements. |
| [Sorbonne Informatics, France](https://sciences.sorbonne-universite.fr/formation-sciences/licences/licences-generales/licence-dinformatique) | Programme pathways and different teaching components. |
| [Maastricht PBL, Netherlands](https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/education/why-um/problem-based-learning) | Course experience and small-group/tutor experience are different. |
| [KTH Systems, Control and Robotics, Sweden](https://www.kth.se/en/studies/master/systems-control-robotics/courses-for-systems-control-and-robotics-1.268326) | Mandatory, track-specific and elective choices. |

These examples are not national rules. Choice varies by institution, degree, year and offering. Choosing a course does not necessarily mean choosing its lecturer.

Store programme context, course, period/group and multiple optional instructors. Required/elective status belongs to a programme/course relationship. A student with a fixed timetable can still benefit from workload, preparation and assessment information.

## Reviews and rankings

Use one review/moderation system with distinct targets:

- **University:** teaching experience, support, facilities, administration and value.
- **Programme/course:** organization, workload, relevance and assessment clarity.
- **Instructor, optional:** explanation clarity, support and teaching experience in context.

Add university comparison and **student-experience rankings**, not purported objective academic/research rankings. Show sample size and recency; withhold ranked scores below a proposed 20 distinct verified contributors per university. Publish a method that does not reward a handful of perfect scores over a large sample. The threshold is a starting proposal, not a representativeness guarantee.

Initially ship reviews and comparison even if no university yet qualifies for a ranked position. Do not invent or seed scores. Keep tiny-group details private.

## The best growth bet to test

1. **Recruit communities before chasing all-EU traffic.** Approach a few willing student societies, programme groups and Erasmus communities across reachable EU campuses. EU availability stays broad; contributions begin concentrated.
2. **Make comparisons worth sharing.** Useful university/programme comparisons can travel through student group chats. Start with simple share links, not a social network.
3. **Build useful localized pages.** Index populated pages that answer actual student questions. Do not expect thousands of empty AI-written university pages to create durable search traffic.
4. **Ask at relevant moments.** Invite reviews after students have experienced teaching; promote comparisons during each institution's application, exchange and course-choice windows.
5. **Keep contribution voluntary.** Readers include prospective students who cannot honestly review yet. Do not require a review to unlock information or reward positive scores.

The proposed loop is: **current students contribute → applicants/exchange students discover and share comparisons → enrolled students use course feedback → some contribute later**. Measure each step; it will not happen automatically.

[StudyCheck](https://www.studycheck.de/) and [EDUopinions](https://www.eduopinions.com/) already serve university/programme review needs. Our advantage must be demonstrated usefulness, trust, contextual detail and distribution, not a claim that this category is empty.

## First growth checkpoint

Proposed 30-day experiment: **100 verified contributors and 150 useful reviews across about five reachable student communities**. These are targets, not forecasts.

Track three things: verified contributors by acquisition source; contexts with enough useful reviews; and readers who return or bring another reader. Measure actual visitors, accounts and monthly active users separately.

If people use and share populated pages, expand the working channels. If not, fix the use case before buying traffic. Millions in year one remains an upside ambition, not a substantiated likelihood.
